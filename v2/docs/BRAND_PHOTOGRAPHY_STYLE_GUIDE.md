# TShirtSwiss Brand Photography Style Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the approved visual direction for all photography used across the TShirtSwiss website, sales materials and future Elementor implementation.

The goal is to make the website feel like one coherent premium apparel-manufacturing brand rather than a collection of unrelated stock images.

## Brand impression

Every image should communicate at least one of the following:

- Swiss-managed reliability
- Premium garment quality
- Real apparel manufacturing capability
- Clear B2B professionalism
- Production transparency
- Product specificity
- International fulfilment readiness

## Core visual character

Approved imagery should feel:

- Authentic
- Precise
- Premium
- Modern
- Technically credible
- Clean but not sterile
- Commercial rather than editorial-fashion driven
- Relevant to the exact page topic

## Colour direction

- Use neutral whites, charcoal, soft greys, natural fabric tones and controlled industrial colours.
- Allow TShirtSwiss red to appear through branding, garments, machinery details or graphic overlays, but do not force red into every photograph.
- Maintain natural skin tones.
- Avoid strong teal-orange grading.
- Avoid oversaturated factory lighting.
- Avoid heavy filters that obscure garment colour accuracy.
- Keep white balance consistent across image groups displayed on the same page.

## Lighting

### Product and detail photography

- Use soft directional light.
- Preserve fabric texture and stitching definition.
- Avoid blown highlights on white garments.
- Avoid crushed shadows on black garments.
- Keep reflective packaging and synthetic fabrics controlled.

### Manufacturing photography

- Use available factory light only when colour rendering remains accurate.
- Supplement with soft light where machinery or garment details would otherwise be lost.
- Show active production rather than empty machinery.
- Keep the workspace credible and organised without making it look artificially staged.

### People and industry photography

- Prefer natural or softly diffused light.
- Show workers, teams and customers in realistic environments.
- Keep garments clearly visible and correctly fitted.
- Avoid images where dramatic lighting hides product detail.

## Composition

### Hero images

- Use landscape images at 16:9 or 2:1.
- Preserve clear negative space for page headings and calls to action.
- Place the main subject opposite the expected text block.
- Keep essential garment features within the central 60% safe area for mobile cropping.
- Avoid placing faces, logos or product details at extreme edges.
- Use one dominant visual idea rather than a busy collage.

### Split-section images

- Use 4:3 images with a clear focal point.
- Frame the subject tightly enough to explain the section without relying on the caption.
- Avoid large empty backgrounds unless they support the page layout.

### Gallery images

- Use a consistent camera height, exposure and crop across each gallery group.
- Mix product, process, detail and application imagery intentionally.
- Do not use three images that communicate the same thing.

### CTA backgrounds

- Use restrained images with low visual complexity.
- Preserve enough tonal consistency for white text.
- Avoid unrelated industrial machinery, office meetings or generic city imagery.
- Dark overlays may be used, but the underlying image must still be relevant.

## Subject rules by page type

### Product pages

Each product page should include:

1. A hero image showing the exact product.
2. A product overview or application image.
3. A manufacturing or branding process image.
4. A detail image showing material, stitching, print, embroidery or label quality.
5. A finished-order or packaging image where appropriate.

The product must remain the visual priority. Do not let backgrounds, models or environments dominate.

### Industry pages

Industry pages must show the relevant people wearing the relevant apparel in the correct environment.

Examples:

- Construction: tradespeople in branded workwear on site.
- Healthcare: clinic staff in coordinated uniforms or scrubs.
- Hospitality: hotel, restaurant or cafe teams wearing uniforms.
- Education: students, staff or teams wearing campus apparel.
- Corporate: employees in coordinated professional clothing.

Do not use empty buildings, equipment-only images or generic meetings as the main visual.

### Service pages

Service pages must show the actual production action, machinery, material or inspection described by the page.

Examples:

- Screen printing: screen, ink, garment and operator.
- Embroidery: machine head, thread and garment.
- Sublimation: transfer, printed panel or finished performance garment.
- Heat transfer: press, transfer film and garment.
- Quality control: measuring, seam inspection, colour check or print inspection.
- Packaging: folding, polybags, tissue, inserts, labels and cartons.

Do not reuse one general factory image across several service pages.

## People and model direction

- Use real working postures and natural expressions.
- Show a credible mix of ages, genders and backgrounds.
- Use models who fit the industry context.
- Clothing must fit correctly and appear production-ready.
- Avoid exaggerated fashion poses.
- Avoid staged handshakes and people pointing at laptops.
- Avoid teams arranged in obviously artificial stock-photo formations.
- Do not use PPE incorrectly in construction or manufacturing imagery.
- Do not use medical apparel in implausible clinical settings.

## Garment presentation

- Keep garments clean, pressed and correctly fitted.
- Show branding placements clearly but do not invent customer trademarks.
- Use neutral placeholder branding or TShirtSwiss-approved mock branding when needed.
- Maintain accurate garment proportions.
- Avoid visible defects unless the image is specifically explaining quality control.
- Keep colour variants consistent across a coordinated collection.
- For technical apparel, show functional features such as stretch panels, closures, pockets and seams.

## Product photography standards

### Flat lay

- Use a clean neutral background.
- Align garments carefully.
- Show front and back when construction differs.
- Include scale or context only when useful.
- Avoid decorative props that distract from the product.

### Mannequin or model

- Use simple poses that reveal garment shape and fit.
- Keep arms from covering logos, pockets or side panels.
- Use front, back and three-quarter views where relevant.

### Detail shots

- Focus on one feature per image.
- Show stitching, embroidery, print texture, label quality, fabric structure or fastening clearly.
- Use enough depth of field to keep the complete feature sharp.

## Manufacturing photography standards

- Show a real production step.
- Include the garment or garment component whenever possible.
- Capture hands, tools and machinery in context.
- Keep safety practices correct.
- Avoid clutter that creates a low-quality impression.
- Avoid wide factory shots where no process can be identified.
- Do not show machinery unrelated to apparel manufacturing.

## Packaging photography standards

- Show folded garments, labels, inserts, tissue, bags, cartons or ecommerce packaging clearly.
- Keep branding consistent.
- Avoid damaged cartons, excessive plastic clutter or untidy packing benches.
- Show shipping labels without exposing personal data.
- Use realistic order quantities for B2B manufacturing.

## Image treatment

- Crop before applying overlays.
- Use moderate contrast.
- Preserve texture in light and dark garments.
- Use subtle desaturation only where needed for visual consistency.
- Do not convert all manufacturing imagery to black and white.
- Do not blur backgrounds so heavily that the production context disappears.
- Do not apply artificial lens flares, fake film grain or strong vignettes.

## Mobile crop rules

- Keep the primary subject within the middle 60% of the source image.
- Keep faces and product logos away from the top and bottom 15%.
- Test at 390 px, 430 px, 768 px and desktop widths.
- Provide alternate mobile crops when one source cannot work across breakpoints.
- Never approve a hero based only on desktop presentation.

## Accessibility and alt text

Alt text must:

- Describe the relevant subject and action.
- Mention the product or service when useful.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Avoid phrases such as `image of` or `photo of`.
- Remain concise.
- Be empty only when an image is purely decorative and conveys no information.

Examples:

- `Operator screen printing a custom logo onto a T-shirt`
- `Healthcare team wearing coordinated custom uniforms`
- `Inspector measuring a finished garment during quality control`
- `Custom woven neck label sewn into a private label garment`

## File standards

- Master source: highest-quality licensed original.
- Delivery formats: AVIF and WebP, with JPG fallback where required.
- Hero target width: 1800-2200 px.
- Content target width: 1000-1400 px.
- Card target width: 800-1000 px.
- Avoid upscaling small stock images.
- Remove unnecessary metadata before publishing.
- Use descriptive lowercase filenames separated by hyphens.

Examples:

- `custom-tshirts-screen-printing-hero.webp`
- `hospitality-team-uniforms.webp`
- `embroidery-machine-logo-detail.webp`
- `quality-control-garment-measurement.webp`

## Prohibited imagery

Do not use:

- Generic office meetings unrelated to garment production
- Business handshakes
- People pointing at screens
- Random warehouse shelves
- Empty factory exteriors
- Unrelated heavy industry
- Aircraft or shipping containers as the sole representation of apparel shipping
- Runway fashion imagery
- Consumer fast-fashion shopping scenes
- Garments where the featured product is obscured
- Incorrect or unsafe PPE
- Low-resolution or visibly compressed stock images
- AI-generated garments with distorted seams, hands, logos or machinery
- Images with unverified commercial rights

## Approval checklist

An image is approved only when all answers are yes:

- Does it match the exact page and section topic?
- Is the garment, process or audience immediately recognisable?
- Does it follow the approved visual style?
- Does it crop correctly on desktop, tablet and mobile?
- Is the licence recorded?
- Is the source resolution sufficient?
- Is the filename descriptive?
- Is the alt text accurate?
- Is the optimised file size acceptable?
- Does it avoid unnecessary repetition elsewhere on the site?
