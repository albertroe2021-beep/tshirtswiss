Premium decoration for long-lasting brand impact

Embroidery is one of the most premium forms of garment decoration. Rather than applying ink to the surface, the design is stitched directly into the garment with thread. This creates visible and tactile structure that looks especially professional on polo shirts, shirts, caps, jackets, uniforms and workwear. For companies, hotels, restaurants, sports clubs, retail brands and private label projects, embroidery creates a durable branded finish that can remain stable through repeated wear and washing.

Why brands choose embroidery

Embroidery communicates value because the branding becomes part of the garment. It is ideal for left-chest logos, sleeve branding, embroidered names, back designs, cap fronts and small premium details. The right approach depends on fabric weight, stretch, design size, number of colours, detail level and the desired effect. A heavyweight polo supports different stitch densities from a lightweight performance shirt, while a structured cap requires different framing and stitch sequencing from a flat jacket pocket.

Commercial embroidery is a technical production process

A strong result begins long before the first machine stitch. The logo must be digitised, separated into stitch types and adjusted for the fabric, position and final size. Underlay, stitch direction, pull compensation, thread tension and stabiliser determine whether outlines stay clean and small lettering remains legible. TShirtSwiss coordinates these technical decisions with garment selection, sample approval, production planning, quality control and packing so the approved embroidery can be repeated consistently across the full order.

Four Embroidery and Patchwork Solutions

The most suitable technique is selected according to the artwork, garment, required depth, application and production quantity.

1

Flat Embroidery

Flat embroidery is the most versatile solution for logos, lettering, names and emblems. It works well on polo shirts, shirts, jackets, uniforms, bags and many cap styles. Satin, fill and running stitches can build clear outlines, solid areas and smaller details. Strong digitising, appropriate stitch density and sufficient design size are essential for clean, legible results.

2

3D Puff Embroidery

For 3D puff, foam is placed beneath selected embroidery areas and fully covered with thread. This creates a raised, dimensional finish that is especially effective on caps, streetwear, hoodies and bold wordmarks. Not every detail is suitable: lines need sufficient width, corners must close cleanly and stitch directions must be planned to cover the foam correctly.

3

Embroidered Patches

Patches are produced separately and then applied to garments, bags or accessories. Options include embroidered areas, woven detail, merrowed borders, laser cutting, hook-and-loop backing, heat-seal backing or traditional sew-on construction. Patches suit collections that repeat the same design across different products or use removable branding elements.

4

Appliqué and Patchwork

Appliqué uses cut fabric shapes positioned on the base material and secured with decorative or functional stitching. This reduces stitch count across large areas and creates additional possibilities using contrast fabric, felt, twill, mesh or reflective material. Patchwork can produce logos, numbers, letters and layered graphic elements with strong visual depth.

Technical Preparation

The artwork must work in thread, on the chosen fabric and at the final size.

A print file cannot simply be sent unchanged to an embroidery machine. Shapes, lines and lettering must be translated into a sequence of physical stitches. The digitiser determines where underlay is required, which areas use satin or fill stitches and how fabric movement will be compensated. Fine lines, tiny lettering and gradients often need simplification or a larger finished size.

The garment also changes the file. Stretch fabrics require different stabilisation from rigid twill. Caps are often embroidered from the centre outwards to control their curved structure. Pockets, seams and zips can restrict hoop access and affect placement. These decisions become visible during the sample stitch-out and are corrected before bulk approval.

  • Logo digitising and stitch sequence
  • Stitch count, density and pull compensation
  • Thread colours, sheen and material
  • Stabiliser, underlay and fabric behaviour
  • Placement, hooping and machine access
  • Sample approval and production reference

Production Management

Consistency across the entire order

After sample approval, the accepted embroidery becomes the reference for bulk production. Thread colours, machine programme, placement, hoop alignment and garment specification are documented. During production, stitch quality, thread tension, outlines, colour changes and positioning are checked regularly so deviations can be identified early.

After embroidery, loose threads are removed, backings are checked and sensitive areas are protected where required. Placement may be adjusted slightly across different garment sizes or product types to maintain consistent visual balance. For repeat orders, stored stitch files and documented approvals help reproduce the same execution.

Frequently Asked Questions About Embroidery

What is the minimum order quantity for embroidery?

The practical minimum depends on the garment, artwork, number of placements and project type. Every new design requires digitising and machine setup. Larger runs spread these costs more efficiently, although smaller projects may still be possible when the product and design are clearly specified. Send us your quantity and requirements for a project-specific assessment.

What does logo digitising mean?

Digitising is the technical conversion of a logo into machine stitches. The process defines stitch types, sequence, density, underlay, thread changes and pull compensation. A normal image file does not contain this information. Good digitising directly affects legibility, outlines, production speed and durability, making it one of the most important stages.

How small can embroidered text be?

The minimum size depends on the typeface, thread, fabric and stitch direction. Very thin lines, tight spacing and small serifs can close up in thread. It is often better to enlarge the lettering, simplify it or create an embroidery-friendly version. A sample stitch-out provides the most reliable confirmation that the text remains readable.

Which garments are suitable for 3D puff embroidery?

3D puff is most often used on structured caps, snapbacks, hoodies and heavyweight streetwear. The design needs sufficiently wide areas and clear shapes so the foam can be completely covered. Tiny details and delicate lettering are less suitable. We can advise which areas should remain flat and which can be raised.

How durable is embroidery when washed?

High-quality machine embroidery is extremely durable when thread, fabric, density and stabilisation are correctly selected. Robust threads and suitable care instructions can be planned for workwear and professional uniforms. Durability also depends on the garment itself; a stable polo or jacket provides a different base from a very lightweight stretch fabric.

Can embroidery be combined with other decoration methods?

Yes. Embroidery can be combined with screen printing, heat transfer, sublimation, woven labels, patches, hang tags and custom packaging. A common solution is a small embroidered chest logo with a larger printed back design. The methods should be planned together so production order, placement and heat exposure are compatible.
